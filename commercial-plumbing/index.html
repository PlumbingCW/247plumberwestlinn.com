When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

A backed-up floor drain in a kitchen, a burst line above a ceiling, or a restroom that cannot be used can force a business to close. Close the nearest shutoff, keep staff and customers out of wet areas and switch off equipment near water. Call right away, since each hour of closure costs more than the repair.

If sewage is involved, stop water use throughout the building and keep the area clear. A health inspector may need to be notified for food service, and the plumber can document the cause.

Backflow prevention

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the clean supply. Businesses with fire sprinklers, irrigation or certain equipment usually have one, and the assembly needs testing on a schedule set by the water provider. A failed test means repair or replacement.

Ask your water provider or the City of West Linn what testing your property requires. The plumber can repair or replace an assembly and coordinate with a certified tester when the test itself must be done by one.

Grease traps and restaurant drains

Kitchen drains carry fats, oils and grease that cool and harden in the line. A grease trap catches most of it, but only if it is serviced on schedule. A full trap sends grease downstream, where it builds up and blocks the line.

Regular trap service and periodic hydro jetting of kitchen lines keep a restaurant open. If a backup has already happened, sewer line cleaning clears the main so you can reopen.

Commercial leak detection

A hidden leak in a commercial building can run for weeks before a stain appears. Leak detection uses acoustic and thermal tools to locate the pipe without opening walls blindly, and it often shows up first as an unexplained rise in the water bill. See leak detection for how the process works.

Keep your business plumbing ready

Simple habits reduce emergency calls.

  • Post the location of the main shutoff where staff can see it.
  • Schedule grease trap service on a regular cycle.
  • Keep backflow test records with your building files.
  • Train staff on what cannot go down kitchen and restroom drains.
  • Keep this number handy: (503) 974-3329.

West Linn businesses and the city rules that apply

The City of West Linn publishes its water quality report, public works contacts and building division guidance. Backflow testing rules come from the water provider, and permit requirements for tenant improvements come from the building division. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of West Linn Building Division before any work that changes plumbing in a commercial space.

West Linn sits on the Willamette River, and heavy rain can load drains in low areas. A commercial floor drain that backs up during a storm may be a public main issue, and a camera check at the cleanout shows whether the blockage is on your side.

What to do before a commercial plumber arrives

Do these while help is on the way.

  • Close the nearest shutoff and keep staff and customers out of wet areas.
  • Switch off equipment near standing water.
  • Stop water use if sewage is rising in a floor drain.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup for your insurer.
  • Have the building address and a contact on site ready for the dispatcher.

Work on commercial plumbing falls under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, the state plumbing code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Which rules apply to your job, and whether a permit is needed, is a question for the plumber and for the City of West Linn Building Division at 503-656-4211.
