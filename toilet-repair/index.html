Emergency Toilet Repair in West Linn, OR

A toilet that overflows, leaks at the base or will not stop running is a problem you want fixed quickly. Toilet repair in West Linn starts with the shutoff valve behind the bowl: turn it clockwise to stop water going in.

Call (503) 974-3329 any hour. A plumber repairs leaks, bad flanges, fill valves and clogs, and installs new toilets when replacement is the better answer.

When a toilet problem is an emergency

A toilet that is overflowing, leaking onto the floor or backing up with other fixtures is urgent. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet. If the valve is stuck, lift the float in the tank to stop the fill and close the main water valve. Mop up water quickly, because moisture soaks into subfloors and seeps through ceilings below.

If the toilet overflows again after plunging, or water appears in a tub or floor drain, the blockage is deeper. Stop using water and call the 24/7 line.

Before the plumber arrives

A few quick steps limit the damage.

  • Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet, turning it clockwise.
  • Lift the tank float or close the main if the valve will not close.
  • Use towels to keep water off subfloors and carpet.
  • Do not flush again until the problem is solved.

Leaks at the base and the flange

Water around the base of a toilet usually means a failed wax ring or a damaged flange, the ring that anchors the toilet to the floor drain. A toilet that rocks, a floor that feels soft or a bad smell after flushing all point to the same area.

A flange that is cracked or sitting below the finished floor can be repaired with a flange repair ring and a new wax seal. A rotted floor around it needs more work, and the plumber tells you what they find before going further.

Running toilets and weak flushes

A toilet that runs is wasting water. The usual culprits are a worn flapper, a fill valve that does not shut off or a chain that is too long. They are inexpensive parts and a quick repair, and fixing them keeps the water bill down.

A weak flush has several causes: clogged rim jets, low tank water level or a partial clog in the trap or drain. If the toilet is older and was never a good performer, replacement with a modern high-efficiency model solves the problem for good.

Toilet installation and replacement

A new toilet is a good move when the old one is cracked, leaks repeatedly or flushes poorly. The plumber removes the old unit, checks the flange and the shutoff valve, sets a new wax seal and connects the new toilet. A new supply line and shutoff valve are inexpensive extras that prevent future leaks.

Rough-in distance, the gap from the wall to the center of the drain, determines which models fit. The plumber measures it before ordering. The related faucet repair page covers the other fixtures in the bathroom.

Why older West Linn bathrooms leak at the toilet

A city survey of the Bolton neighborhood found that 250 of the 418 older properties recorded were built in the 1950s and 1960s. Bathrooms in those homes have often been updated over the decades, and each update can leave a toilet set on a flange that was never lowered or raised to match new flooring.

Tile or vinyl added over an old floor can raise the floor above the flange, which leaves the wax seal without enough compression. A flange extension or repair ring fixes it, and it is a common repair on older homes in Bolton, Willamette and Sunset.
