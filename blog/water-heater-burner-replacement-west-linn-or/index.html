Water Heater Burner Replacement for West Linn, OR Homes

A gas water heater with a failing burner assembly may not light, may light and go out or may produce weak heat. In some cases the burner, thermocouple or gas control can be replaced. In others the tank is old enough that replacement is the better value.

This guide explains the symptoms, what the repair involves and how to decide. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside, then call (503) 974-3329 for the plumber.

By the West Linn 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026 · West Linn, OR

Call (503) 974-3329

Safety first: gas smell and pilot trouble

If you smell gas, do not touch light switches or the heater. Leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. A pilot that will not stay lit is not just inconvenient. It can mean the thermocouple is failing or the flue is not drawing, and either can create a safety problem.

Only a licensed professional should replace a burner assembly or gas control, since a wrong connection can leak gas. This guide is to help you understand the work, not to do it yourself.

Signs the burner or its parts are failing

Symptoms that point to the burner

A tech checks these in order.

  • The pilot lights but the main burner will not ignite.
  • The flame is yellow, floppy or uneven instead of steady blue.
  • Soot or scorching around the burner or draft hood.
  • The heater runs for a long time but water stays lukewarm.
  • The pilot keeps going out after it is relit.

A weak or yellow flame often means the burner is dirty or the air supply is restricted. A clean-and-adjust may fix that. A cracked burner tube or a failed thermocouple needs replacement.

What the repair involves

The plumber shuts off the gas and water supply, removes the burner assembly, inspects the thermocouple or flame sensor and the pilot, and installs matching parts. The new assembly is tested with a flame check and a leak test on every gas connection.

Sediment in the tank can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the burner's life, so the plumber also checks the tank. A flush may be part of the visit. If the tank is leaking or rusted, a burner is not worth installing.

West Linn's permit form and your water heater

The City of West Linn Building Division's plumbing permit application lists a water heater as a fixture line item, and asks the contractor for a Construction Contractors Board license and a plumbing board license. The Building Division is at 22500 Salamo Road and can be reached at 503-656-4211.

What this means for you: replacing a water heater is a permit conversation, and a plumber who knows the city should raise it. A simple burner repair may be treated differently, so ask the Building Division what applies to your job. Also, the city's water report recommends keeping the heater at or below 120 degrees Fahrenheit and advises avoiding hot water during neighborhood main flushing to keep sediment out of the tank.

Source type: City of West Linn Building Division plumbing permit form and annual water quality report

Cost and timing

A burner or thermocouple repair is usually less than a new heater, but not if the tank is near the end of its life. Ask the plumber for both numbers, and ask what the age and condition of the tank say about the next five years.

A no-hot-water call is an emergency for many households. The line is open 24/7, and same-day service is often possible.

What to expect on the visit

The plumber looks at the heater age on the data plate, checks the venting and draft, inspects the burner and tests the gas connections. You get a plain explanation of what failed and what each option costs, including whether replacement makes more sense than a repair.

If the heater is in a closet or crawlspace, clear the path before the visit. Note the symptoms in order, such as when the pilot goes out and how long the water stays hot, since that helps the plumber find the cause.
