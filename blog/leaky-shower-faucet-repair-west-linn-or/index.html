How to Repair a Leaky Shower Faucet: A West Linn, OR Homeowner Guide

A shower that drips after you turn it off is almost always a worn cartridge, washer or valve seat. Most single handle valves hold a replaceable cartridge, and swapping it fixes the drip in many homes.

This guide walks through what causes the leak, how the repair works, what can go wrong and when to bring in a plumber. If water will not shut off, close the main valve and call (503) 974-3329.

By the West Linn 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026 · West Linn, OR

Call (503) 974-3329

Where the water is really coming from

A shower faucet has three possible leak points. A drip from the shower head means the valve inside the wall is not sealing. A drip from the tub spout while the shower runs points to a diverter. A leak around the handle means worn O-rings or a loose stem.

Knowing which one you have saves effort. Water at the handle can usually be fixed from the front, while a failed valve seat may need access behind the tile.

Shower leak causes at a glance
What you seeLikely causeUsual fix
Head drips after shutoffWorn cartridge or seatReplace cartridge
Water leaks around the handleWorn O-rings or packing nutReplace O-rings and tighten
Tub spout runs with shower onFailed diverterReplace spout or diverter
Water will not turn off at allFailed valve bodyReplace valve

How the cartridge swap works

First turn off the water at the stop valve or the main. Open the faucet to drain the line. Remove the handle and trim, and then the retaining clip or nut that holds the cartridge. Pull the cartridge straight out, and match the new one to the brand and model, since cartridges are not interchangeable.

Clean the valve body before the new cartridge goes in, apply plumber's grease to the seals, and reassemble. Turn the water on slowly and test hot and cold. A cartridge that will not budge needs a puller, and forcing it can break the valve.

Before you start

A few steps prevent a bigger mess.

  • Shut off the water and confirm the shower is dead.
  • Photograph each step so you can reassemble in order.
  • Plug the tub drain so small parts do not fall in.
  • Have the brand and model of the valve ready, found on the trim or inside the valve.

When it is a plumber job

If the stop valves do not shut off, if the cartridge is seized, or if the valve body is corroded, stop. The valve is behind the wall, and a wrong move can crack it or damage tile. A plumber has the pullers and valve bodies and can open the wall neatly if needed.

Also call if the handle will not stop turning, the water changes temperature suddenly or the leak is inside the wall. See faucet repair for how we handle shower valves and tub spouts.

West Linn's temperature advice for your hot water

The City of West Linn's water quality report recommends keeping a water heater at or below 120 degrees Fahrenheit. A shower valve that has worn out can let temperature swing, and hot water at the wrong moment is a scald risk, so a cartridge that has lost its balance is worth replacing even if the leak seems small.

The same report describes the city's water as soft, with a 2024 hardness of 36.0 ppm. Soft water leaves less scale on shower heads and valve seats, so a cartridge here usually fails from wear rather than mineral buildup. If you see white crust at the head, clean it and check the water heater setting.

Source type: City of West Linn annual water quality report (2024 data)

Cost and what changes it

A cartridge swap is a modest repair. Costs rise when the valve is a rare brand, when the body is damaged, when the wall must be opened or when tile has to be repaired. Ask the plumber to explain whether the work is a repair or a valve replacement, and what happens if the wall must be opened.

If the shower is already in a remodel plan, replacing the valve at the same time is worth discussing, since the wall is open.

Why a small shower drip adds up

A shower head that drips once a second does not sound serious, but it runs all day and all night. Over a month that is a lot of water, and over a year it adds to the water and sewer bill. The drip also keeps a valve body damp, which speeds corrosion on older brass and encourages mildew on the wall behind the trim.

If you notice a smell or a stain on the ceiling below a second floor bathroom, treat it as a possible leak inside the wall and shut off the water to the shower. A small repair now avoids opening tile later.

Same valves in nearby towns

The same valves are in homes in Oregon City and Tualatin, and the same steps apply. Remodeled bathrooms often have a newer trim over an old valve body, which means the cartridge you need may be for the old valve, not the new handle. Take a photo of the valve before you buy parts.
