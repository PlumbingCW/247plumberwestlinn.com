Power Vent Water Heater Installation: A West Linn, OR Homeowner Guide

A power vent water heater has a fan that pushes exhaust out through plastic pipe, so it can vent through a side wall instead of a tall chimney. That makes it a good fit when a house has no usable flue.

This guide explains how it works, what an installation involves, what to ask about permits and what a quote should list. For a failed heater tonight, call (503) 974-3329.

By the West Linn 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026 · West Linn, OR

Call (503) 974-3329

How a power vent heater differs from a standard one

A standard gas heater relies on natural draft. Hot exhaust rises through a metal flue to the roof. A power vent heater adds an electric blower, so exhaust is pushed out through PVC or similar pipe that can run horizontally to an exterior wall.

The blower needs an outlet nearby, and a failed blower or pressure switch stops the heater from firing. The trade is flexibility for one more part that needs power.

What the installation involves

Steps in a power vent installation

The order a plumber follows.

  • Drain and remove the old heater and disconnect the gas, water and flue.
  • Set the new heater, level it and connect water lines with a pan or drain where needed.
  • Run the gas line with a leak test on each joint.
  • Install the vent and combustion air pipes and seal each joint.
  • Connect the power, fill, purge air and light the heater.
  • Test the flame, the blower and the relief valve discharge.

The venting run is where the work is. Length, number of elbows and termination point are set by the manufacturer's instructions, and the exit has to be clear of windows, doors and other openings. A plumber measures the route before quoting.

What a good quote lists

Ask for the heater model and warranty, the venting materials, any new gas or electrical work, a drain or drain pan, a new relief valve line, the permit and the haul-away of the old tank. A quote that bundles everything into one number makes it hard to compare.

Also ask what happens if the existing venting or gas line is not usable. The answer should be a price, not a surprise. Our water heater installation page lists what a visit includes.

West Linn permit contacts for a water heater job

The City of West Linn Building Division is at 22500 Salamo Road, and its public phone is 503-656-4211. Its plumbing permit form lists a water heater as a fixture line item and asks the contractor to list both a Construction Contractors Board license and a plumbing board license.

For you, that means two practical steps. Ask the plumber who pulls the permit and when the inspection will be scheduled, and ask to see the license numbers listed on the application. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Building Division what applies to a power vent installation in your home.

What to do if the heater smells or sounds wrong

Safety steps come before troubleshooting.

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.
  • If you smell exhaust or see soot, switch the heater off and open windows.
  • Do not touch the vent joints while the heater is hot.
  • Do not relight a heater that has shut itself off more than once.
  • Call (503) 974-3329 and describe the sound, the smell and the display code.

Common problems after an installation

If the heater will not light, the blower, the pressure switch or the venting may be at fault. A kinked or blocked vent triggers the safety switch and shuts the heater down. If you smell exhaust, shut the heater off and call, since a loose vent joint is a safety problem.

A heater that is noisy or short cycles may have a vent that is too long or has too many bends. That is a design issue, not a part failure, and the installer should correct it.

Maintenance that keeps a power vent heater running

Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment, and test the relief valve by lifting the lever briefly, with a bucket below the discharge pipe. Look at the vent joints for loose connections and the outside termination for nests or debris. Keep the area around the heater clear, because the unit pulls combustion air.

If you hear the blower labor, see the pilot indicator code flash or smell exhaust, call a plumber rather than resetting the heater repeatedly.

Keep the owner's manual and the installation receipt together. The model and serial number speed up warranty claims, and the plumber can order the right blower or pressure switch on the first try if you can read the data plate.

Plumbing work in Oregon follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). This guide is general information. For your own house, ask the plumber which rules apply and check permits with the City of West Linn Building Division.
