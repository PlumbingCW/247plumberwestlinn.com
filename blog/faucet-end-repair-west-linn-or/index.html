Faucet End Repair for West Linn, OR Homes

The end of a faucet is where water leaves, and it can fail in a few ways. An aerator can clog or crack, a spout can drip from a worn cartridge, and an outdoor hose bib can leak at the spout or the handle.

This guide covers each, with simple fixes and the points where a plumber is better. For a hose bib that will not shut off, close the main and call (503) 974-3329.

Indoor spout ends and aerators

The aerator is the small screen at the tip of a kitchen or bathroom faucet. It mixes air into the stream and catches sediment. When it clogs, the flow sprays sideways or drops, and it can leak around the threads if the washer inside is worn.

Unscrew it by hand or with a cloth and pliers, rinse the screen, and replace the washer if it is cracked. A new aerator is a quick, low cost fix. If the spout itself drips with the handle off, the cartridge is worn.

How to clean an aerator

Five minutes, no special tools.

  • Close the faucet and place a cloth in the sink to catch parts.
  • Unscrew the aerator counterclockwise.
  • Take it apart and rinse out grit with water.
  • Replace a cracked washer or screen.
  • Screw it back on and run the water to check for leaks.

Outdoor hose bibs

A hose bib has a washer that wears and a stem packing that leaks at the handle. A drip from the spout when the valve is closed means a failed washer. A drip at the handle means the packing nut needs tightening or the packing needs replacing.

A hose bib that is left connected to a hose in winter can freeze and split the pipe behind the wall. The break may not show until spring, when the water is turned on and a wet patch appears inside.

West Linn's warning about hoses and backflow

The City of West Linn's water quality report warns that garden hoses, especially hoses submerged in pools or attached to chemical sprayers, can cause cross-connection contamination, and that backflow prevention devices protect the system. It also notes that improperly installed toilet valves can create the same hazard.

The practical step for a hose bib is a backflow preventer, often a small vacuum breaker that screws onto the spout. If your bib has none, a plumber can add one or replace the bib with a frost-proof model that includes one. Do not leave a hose submerged or attached to a sprayer under pressure.

Source type: City of West Linn annual water quality report (2024 data)

Frost-proof bibs and winter care

A frost-proof hose bib has a long stem that puts the valve seat inside the warm part of the wall. It still needs care. Disconnect the hose in autumn, since a connected hose traps water in the bib. Check that the bib slopes slightly downward so it drains.

If a pipe has frozen, open the faucet and warm the pipe slowly. Do not use an open flame. If it is split, close the main before it thaws, and call.

When to call a plumber

Call if the bib leaks inside the wall, if the stem is corroded, if the faucet body is cracked or if you cannot match a cartridge. Our faucet repair page covers fixtures, and the burst pipe repair page covers a frozen line. The plumber can also replace a worn outdoor bib and add the backflow device in the same visit.

A short fall maintenance routine

Walk the house each autumn with a notepad. Disconnect hoses, check each outdoor bib for drips, open the interior shutoff to any bib that has one and drain the line. Clean aerators on kitchen and bathroom faucets while you are at it, since sediment from a season of use collects there.

Note any faucet that drips, feels loose or has a stiff handle. Small problems are cheaper to fix before winter than during a freeze.

If a handle has become stiff, a little silicone grease on the stem often frees it. If it will not turn at all, do not force it, since a snapped stem turns a small job into a pipe repair. Also look at the pipe where it enters the house and make sure insulation or a foam cover protects any exposed run.
