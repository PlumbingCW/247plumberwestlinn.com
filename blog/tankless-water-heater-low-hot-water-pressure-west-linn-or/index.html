Low Hot Water Pressure on a Tankless Heater: A West Linn, OR Guide

If your hot water runs weak but your cold water is fine, the cause is often in the tankless heater or the hot side piping. The usual suspects are a clogged inlet filter, a scaled heat exchanger, a partly closed valve or a fixture aerator full of debris.

This guide walks through checks from simplest to most involved, and when to call. If hot water is out entirely, call (503) 974-3329 any hour.

By the West Linn 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026 · West Linn, OR

Call (503) 974-3329

Start with the easy checks

Compare hot and cold at the same faucet. If cold is strong and hot is weak, the problem is on the hot side. If both are weak at one fixture, the aerator or a supply line is the cause. If both are weak at every fixture, it may be a whole house pressure issue.

Then try a second faucet. If only one fixture is weak, clean its aerator. If every hot tap is weak, look at the heater.

Checks in order

Do these before opening the heater.

  • Clean the aerator on the weak faucet.
  • Check that the heater isolation valves are fully open.
  • Look at the heater display for an error code.
  • Test hot flow at a second fixture.
  • Check whether the water softener or a filter bypass is set correctly.

The inlet filter and the heat exchanger

Most tankless heaters have a small inlet screen that catches grit. After plumbing work or a main flush, the screen can clog and cut the flow to the heater. The manufacturer's manual shows how to remove and rinse it, and a plumber can do it in minutes.

Scale inside the heat exchanger is the second cause. Minerals narrow the passages and reduce flow, and the unit may also overheat. Flushing the unit with a descaling solution fixes most cases. See our tankless water heater page for service options.

West Linn's advice on aerators and main flushing

The City of West Linn's water quality report says to clean faucet screens and aerators regularly. It also says that during neighborhood main flushing you should avoid using tap water for household uses if possible. If you must, run cold water at full velocity for a few minutes and avoid hot water, which prevents sediment from building up in the hot water tank.

For a tankless owner, the same logic applies to the inlet filter. After a flushing event, sediment can reach the heater, so check the inlet screen if hot flow drops. The same report lists hardness at 36.0 ppm, which is soft, so scale is less likely than debris to be the cause here.

Source type: City of West Linn annual water quality report (2024 data)

When the heater is not the problem

A weak flow can also come from a partly closed shutoff, a kinked supply line, a failed pressure reducing valve or a clogged pipe. Galvanized supply pipe in an older house narrows with rust, and the hot side shows it first because it is heated. If pressure is low everywhere, the cause is upstream of your heater.

A plumber can measure pressure at a hose bib to tell whether the problem is in your house or in the supply.

When to call

Call if there is an error code you cannot clear, if the unit leaks, if water is lukewarm even at low flow or if the screen is clean and flow is still weak. If the heater is not firing at all, the water heater repair page lists the next steps.

A recurring problem after every main flush deserves a conversation about a whole-house sediment filter, which protects the heater.

Sizing: when the unit is not broken, just overworked

A tankless heater has a maximum flow rate for a given temperature rise. In cold months the incoming water is colder, so the same unit delivers less hot water at the same setting. Running two showers and the dishwasher together can exceed what it can heat, and hot flow then falls or the temperature drops.

If that is the pattern, staggering use solves it. If it happens every day, a larger unit or a second unit may be the lasting answer, and a plumber can size it from your fixtures and your winter inlet temperature.
