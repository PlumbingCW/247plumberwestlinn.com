Soft water and what it does to your fixtures

Tualatin buys treated water from the City of Portland, and its 2023 report describes Portland's main source as the Bull Run Watershed. Bull Run water is soft, with total hardness stated as 3 to 8 parts per million. Portland's groundwater, used at times, is about 80 parts per million.

Soft water leaves little scale in water heaters and fixtures, so a Tualatin water heater usually fails from age and tank corrosion rather than buildup. If your heater is past a decade, plan a replacement before it leaks.

A river town that grew in stages

The city's history page says Tualatin was platted in 1886 around a railway depot, that the Oregon Electric line reached town in 1906 and ran along Boone's Ferry Road, and that the city incorporated in 1913. Newer subdivisions followed over the decades, so a single street can hold houses from several eras.

That mix means different pipes in different homes: older houses may have galvanized or early copper lines, while newer ones have PEX. A repair call starts with the age of the house, since it tells the plumber what to expect.

What the city report means for your plumbing

The 2023 report states a total chlorine residual running average of 0.84 to 2.06 ppm across the distribution system, against a limit of 4 ppm. Chlorine at those levels does not harm pipes, but high chlorine can wear rubber seals over time.

Soft, slightly alkaline water is gentle on piping, which is why a Tualatin leak is more likely to come from an old fitting than from corrosion. The report is published by the city each year if you want the full tables.
