Bolton is the riverside neighborhood at the south end of West Linn, where Willamette Drive runs past the falls and the locks. Its housing is mostly mid-century, so many Bolton homes are old enough to have been repaired, updated or partly remodeled more than once.

Where Bolton sits

The City of West Linn describes Bolton as sitting along the Willamette River at the foot of the falls. A 2010 historic resources survey puts its edges at Mark Lane to the north, Skyline Drive to the west and the Willamette River to the south and east. The survey left out the Holly Grove and Buck Street areas because they had been surveyed before.

The survey covered about 600 acres. Boundaries on a map can differ from how neighbors describe the area, so we go by the street address you give us.

What the city survey says about Bolton homes

The survey recorded 418 properties built in 1964 or earlier, and 415 of them are residences. Most were built in the 1950s (29 percent) and 1960s (31 percent), with smaller groups from the 1920s, 1930s and 1940s. Ranch houses were the most common type, with 192 of the 418.

A group of about 82 mostly late 1950s and early 1960s houses along Mark Lane, Munger Drive, Lowell Avenue, La Fave Street and part of Jolie Pointe Road was identified as a possible mid-century modern historic district.

What this means for plumbing: the survey records style and age, not pipe materials. A house of that era may have original piping, newer piping or a mix after remodels, and only a look at the actual house tells you which. A plumber can check the pipe at the water heater, under sinks and at the meter.

Source type: City of West Linn historic resources survey of Bolton and Sunset, 2010

Plumbing jobs we are asked about in mid-century homes

General examples, not a claim about any one street.

  • A water heater that has reached the end of its life. See water heater replacement.
  • A kitchen or bath drain that clogs more than once. See drain cleaning.
  • A faucet or shower valve worn from years of use. See faucet repair.
  • A wet spot, a high water bill or a running meter. See leak detection.
  • A remodel that moves a kitchen or bathroom and needs new supply or drain lines. See plumbing installation.

Hills, forest and old trees

The city plan for the area speaks of keeping the forested hills in Bolton free of intrusive development, and many Bolton lots have mature trees. Tree roots can enter a sewer lateral at a joint, but not every slow drain means roots. A camera look through sewer inspection shows what is in the line before anyone digs.

Near the falls: business and homes together

Willamette Drive and Willamette Falls Drive carry shops, the library, the post office and the paper mill area right beside homes. If you run a small business here, see commercial plumbing for grease, backflow and restroom issues. Neighbors in Willamette and Sunset call the same number.
