Rain, groundwater and your sewer lateral

Clackamas County's Water Environment Services explains that inflow and infiltration is rainwater or groundwater seeping into sewer lines through damaged pipes or improper connections, and that it raises the risk of overflows. Its stated goal is to cut it by 65 percent in key basins by 2040. That is a county-scale program, but the cause is often at the household level: a cracked lateral or a bad connection.

If your drains run slow only after heavy rain, groundwater may be entering the lateral. A camera inspection shows cracks and joint damage, and sewer line repair fixes them.

What the county and city publish about sewers

The Clackamas County release says WES reimburses 33 percent of the repair costs a city incurs to reduce inflow and infiltration in city-owned pipes, and that the program is meant to save about $120 million in upgrades. It describes city-owned pipes and county treatment as separate systems.

What this means for a homeowner: a repair on your own lateral is your cost, and the city and county programs cover public pipes. Ask the City of Gladstone before any repair in the street or right of way.
