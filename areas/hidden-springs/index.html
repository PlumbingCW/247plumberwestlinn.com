A ridge neighborhood with creeks

What the neighborhood plan documents

The Hidden Springs Neighborhood Plan says the neighborhood covers 478 acres in northwest West Linn on a ridge above the Willamette and Tualatin Rivers. Slopes are moderate to steep, ridge elevations average 550 feet and the highest point in the city, 743 feet, is here. Its creeks are Fern, Robin, Trillium, Heron and Hidden Springs Creeks.

The plan's 2005 estimate was 3,105 residents and 1,280 housing units, of which 1,006 were single-family. The rest were multifamily. There are no commercial properties. It describes storm drainage as in variable condition and says several sewer segments needed upgrades. These are city systems and do not describe the pipes inside your home.

What this means for plumbing: on a hillside, water pressure varies by elevation and the run from the street. A plumber can test pressure and check the regulator.

Source type: City of West Linn Hidden Springs Neighborhood Plan, 2005 estimates

Common calls on hillside homes

General examples, not a claim about any one street.

  • Pressure that is too high on a lower lot or too low on a higher lot. See faucet repair and leak detection.
  • A water heater that has stopped working. See water heater replacement.
  • A drain that slows after heavy rain. See drain cleaning.
  • A kitchen refresh with a new disposal. See garbage disposal repair.
  • An upgrade to a tankless unit. See tankless water heaters.

Streets and neighbors

Rosemont Road, Santa Anita Drive and Hidden Springs Road are the minor arterials in the plan, and Carriage Way, Pimlico Drive, Summit Street and Horton Road are collector streets. Homeowners in Robinwood and nearby streets call the same line.

Neighbors in Parker Crest call the same line.
