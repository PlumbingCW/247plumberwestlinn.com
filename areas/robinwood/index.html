What the neighborhood plan says

The City of West Linn adopted the Robinwood Neighborhood Plan on May 12, 2008. It places the neighborhood along Willamette Drive (Highway 43) with Willamette River frontage and Mary S. Young State Park next door. It describes hillside areas above the highway as a forested backdrop and calls the commercial area along Willamette Drive the Robinwood Main Street.

The plan lists streets such as Robinwood Way, Walling Way, Cedaroak Drive, Mapleton Drive, Kenthorpe Drive, Lazy River Drive and Marylhurst Drive. It describes the neighborhood as mostly single-family. It gives no counts or ages for houses, so we do not state any.

What this means for plumbing: a hillside lot can mean a long run from the street to the house, and slope changes water pressure. A plumber can test pressure and check the pressure reducing valve if one is installed.

Source type: City of West Linn Robinwood Neighborhood Plan, 2008

Water and drainage notes

The 2008 plan mentions a Lake Oswego water treatment facility on Kenthorpe Drive and a need to replace Lake Oswego water pipelines along Robinwood streets. It also notes a covered ditch on Marylhurst Drive. The plan does not say which houses get which water. Check your water bill to see who supplies yours.

If a pipe fails inside your house, close the main valve and call. Our burst pipe repair page lists the first steps.

Common calls for hillside homes and small businesses

General examples, not a claim about any one address.

  • Low or uneven water pressure. See faucet repair and leak detection.

  • A tankless upgrade for a growing family. See tankless water heaters.
