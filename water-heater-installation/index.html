Planning the installation

A planned install gives you the one thing an emergency never does: time to choose. Start with how much hot water the household uses at the same moment. Two showers and a dishwasher running together is a different load from one person on a weekday morning.

Then look at where the heater sits. A garage, a utility closet and a crawlspace each affect what can be installed and how much work is involved. Venting, drain access and clearance for service are as important as the tank size.

Gas, electric and hybrid installs

Gas heaters need correct venting and combustion air, a proper gas connection and a drip leg, and the plumber tests every joint for leaks before the unit is put to work. Electric heaters need the right circuit and disconnect, and the breaker rating must match the heater.

Hybrid heat pump models pull heat from the surrounding air, so the room needs enough volume and airflow. They are loud enough that you will notice one near a bedroom wall. A condensate line has to run to a drain or pump.

What to have ready before an install visit

A few details help the plumber quote accurately before arriving.

  • Photo of the old heater label showing capacity, fuel and model.
  • Where the heater sits: garage, closet, attic or crawlspace.
  • Whether the home uses gas, electric or both.
  • Any plans for a remodel that may change plumbing in the next year.

Expansion tanks, relief valves and safety parts

A closed plumbing system needs a place for expanding hot water to go. Where the water supply has a backflow preventer or pressure regulator, an expansion tank is usually part of the installation. The temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge line are safety parts and are installed or replaced with the heater.

Seismic strapping keeps a tank from tipping, which matters in Oregon. The plumber checks local requirements and installs the strap kit when the installation calls for it.

Permits and codes for an installation in West Linn

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of West Linn Building Division what applies to your address. Oregon's plumbing code is the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code. Work in Oregon has to be done by people authorized by law, and contractors need both a Construction Contractors Board license and the matching Building Codes Division plumbing contractor license.

Both agencies offer an online license search, and it takes a minute to check any plumber you hire, including us. Savanna Oaks and other newer West Linn homes often have garage or closet heater locations that are easy to work with, while older homes in Bolton and Willamette may need venting or access corrections.

What to do before installation day

Do these while help is on the way.

  • Clear a path to the heater and a few feet of working room around it.
  • Note where the main water valve and the gas shutoff are.
  • Ask whether a permit and inspection are part of the quote.
  • Plan for the water to be off for a few hours.
  • If you smell gas at any time, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.

Work on water heater installation falls under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, the state plumbing code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
