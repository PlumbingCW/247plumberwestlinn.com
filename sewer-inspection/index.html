Sewer Camera Inspection in West Linn, OR

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible rod through your line and shows what is inside on a live screen. It finds cracks, roots, belly sections, offsets and blockages without digging.

West Linn sewer inspection is a planned service, and the phone line is open 24/7 if a backup turns it into an emergency. Call (503) 974-3329 to schedule, and the plumber explains what the camera finds in plain terms.

Call (503) 974-3329

What a sewer camera shows

The camera records the pipe wall as it travels, and a locator at the surface marks the depth and position of any problem. The plumber looks for root entry at joints, cracks, offsets where sections have shifted, belly sections that hold water, scale buildup and the pipe material itself.

You see the footage as it happens, and you can keep a recording. That is useful for deciding between cleaning, lining or digging, and it makes quotes from other contractors comparable.

When to schedule an inspection

The best time is before a problem becomes an emergency: after a second backup, when drains gurgle, when a tree has grown near the line, or before buying a house with an older lateral. A camera costs less than a surprise excavation, and it can save a buyer from inheriting a failing line.

It also helps after a cleaning. If the line has been cleared and you want to know why it clogged, the inspection answers that. Our sewer line cleaning page explains the cleaning side.

Good times for a camera look

Consider scheduling one if any of these apply.

  • The line has clogged more than once in a year.
  • You are buying a home built before 1980.
  • A mature tree grows near the lateral.
  • Drains gurgle or smell after heavy rain.
  • You plan a major remodel that adds fixtures to an old drain system.

Before you buy a West Linn home

A standard home inspection rarely includes the sewer lateral. A camera inspection on a house built in the 1950s or 1960s is a smart step. The recording shows the condition of the pipe that will carry your waste for the next decades, and it gives you a solid basis for negotiating a repair credit.

Homes in Bolton, Willamette and Sunset are often several decades old, with mature trees close to the lateral. A camera can show whether the pipe is original, whether it has been lined and whether roots are a concern.

What the city publishes that helps you read the footage

The City of West Linn publishes a lead service line inventory, an annual water quality report and a historic resources survey that includes the age of homes by neighborhood. In Sunset, the survey counted 252 older properties, with 63 built in the 1950s and 48 in the 1940s. That age range tells you what pipe material is likely to show up on camera.

The camera shows the sewer side, while the inventory covers the water service line. They are different pipes, and a plumber can explain which is which. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of West Linn Building Division before any repair that involves excavation.

Source type: City of West Linn water quality report, lead service line inventory page and Sunset historic survey

What affects the cost of a camera inspection

  • AccessA cleanout at the foundation is the easiest. Without one, the plumber enters through a toilet or roof vent.
  • Line lengthLonger runs take more time.
  • Pre-cleaningA line with heavy debris may need clearing before the camera can pass.
  • LocatingMarking the depth and position on the surface adds time.
  • RecordingA saved recording and report are part of the visit.

A camera inspection is a planned cost, not an insurance claim, though some insurers want one after a loss. Want your real number? Call (503) 974-3329.

Work on sewer inspection falls under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, the state plumbing code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Which rules apply to your job, and whether a permit is needed, is a question for the plumber and for the City of West Linn Building Division at 503-656-4211.
