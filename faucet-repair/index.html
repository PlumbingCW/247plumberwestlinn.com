Emergency Faucet Repair in West Linn, OR

A dripping spout is a nuisance. A faucet, supply line or valve that will not shut off is a flooding risk. Faucet repair in West Linn starts at the small oval or round valve under the fixture: turn it clockwise to stop the water, and use the main valve if it will not close.

Call (503) 974-3329 any hour for emergency fixture leaks. The plumber repairs faucets, shower valves, tub spouts and supply stops, and replaces the fixture when parts are no longer worth repairing.

When a fixture leak is urgent

A fixture becomes urgent when water cannot be stopped or is running somewhere it should not: a supply line that has split under a sink, a stop valve that sprays when touched, a tub spout that gushes with the shower off. Close the stop valve first. If it will not close, shut off the main water valve for the house.

Then open a lower faucet to drain the line and relieve pressure. Mop the cabinet, because water behind a cabinet panel soaks into the wall and subfloor and starts the clock on mold.

Dripping faucets and why they happen

A drip comes from a worn cartridge, washer, O-ring or seat inside the faucet body. Each drip is small, but a steady drip can add a noticeable amount to a month of water use. Ceramic disc and cartridge faucets drip when the seals wear. Compression faucets drip when the washer wears.

Repair or replace depends on age and finish. A good-quality faucet with a worn cartridge is worth repairing. A faucet with a pitted finish, loose handle and discontinued parts is usually better replaced.

Shower valves, tub spouts and supply stops

A shower valve that will not shut off or switches temperature is a cartridge or valve body problem. Access is through the trim or sometimes a panel behind the wall, and an older valve may need replacement. A tub spout with a diverter that fails sends water down the spout while the shower is on.

Old supply stops under sinks and toilets often seize or leak when turned. They are inexpensive to replace and good insurance against the day you need them. We recommend replacing stops during a faucet replacement when they are decades old.

Know where your shutoffs are

Five minutes now saves a flooded cabinet later.

  • Find the stop valves under each sink and behind each toilet.
  • Find the main water valve and make sure it turns.
  • Find the water heater shutoff on the cold supply line.
  • Test each valve once a year and exercise it so it does not seize.

Pressure and fixture wear in West Linn homes

West Linn's elevation changes shape water pressure. The Sunset neighborhood rises to about 620 feet near Summit and Knox streets, and homes lower on slopes run higher pressure than homes near the top. Sustained pressure above what a house is designed for wears cartridges, supply lines and fill valves early.

If you replace the same fixture parts repeatedly, ask the plumber to test pressure at a hose bib. The 2024 water quality report lists chlorine at 0.94 parts per million and hardness at 36, so mineral buildup on aerators is slower here than in hard-water areas, and a persistent clog in an aerator usually means sediment from older piping.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Do these while help is on the way.

  • Close the stop valve under the fixture, or the main water valve if it will not close.
  • Open a lower faucet to relieve pressure in the line.
  • Dry the cabinet and photograph any damage for your insurer.
  • Do not force a stuck valve, since a broken stop can flood the cabinet.
  • Keep (503) 974-3329 handy and tell the dispatcher what you shut off.
