Whole House Repiping in West Linn, OR

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry water through your house. It is the right call when pipes leak repeatedly, water runs brown or pressure keeps falling, and patching one section only moves the failure down the line.

Call (503) 974-3329 to plan a repipe. The plumber inspects the system, explains the options in plain terms and gives you a price before work starts. The line is open 24/7 if a failing pipe turns into an emergency.

Signs your pipes are at the end of their life

The first sign is usually a leak, then a second leak in a different spot. Pinholes in copper, weeping joints in galvanized steel and brittle fittings all point to a system that is wearing out as a whole. Rusty or discolored water, low flow at several fixtures and a metallic taste are the other common signals.

One repair is normal maintenance. Three repairs in a couple of years usually means the next one is coming, and each one can damage walls and floors before you notice it.

Reasons to consider a repipe

Several of these together are a strong signal.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks or joint failures.
  • Brown or orange water when a tap first opens.
  • Low pressure at fixtures that used to run strong.
  • Galvanized steel pipe in a home built before the 1960s.
  • Plans for a remodel that will open the walls anyway.

PEX, copper and what each costs you

PEX is flexible plastic tubing. It runs through walls with fewer joints, resists corrosion and handles a cold snap better than rigid pipe because it can expand slightly. Copper is rigid metal with a long track record, and it is a good choice where a house already has copper and a section is being extended.

The plumber recommends a material based on the house, the water and your plans. Whichever you choose, the work is done to the Oregon plumbing code and inspected where a permit is required.

How a repipe works day to day

The plumber maps the system, shuts off water and opens small access holes rather than tearing out whole walls. New lines run in a different route when that is easier, and the old pipe is abandoned or removed. Water is restored in stages so you are not without it for days.

Drywall patches follow the plumbing, and the plumber explains who handles finish work before the job starts. Ask about timing, since most houses can be done in a few days depending on size and access.

Why West Linn homes are repipe candidates

The city's history page shows West Linn grew from 2,923 people in 1960 to over 7,000 in 1970, and the Bolton and Sunset surveys count many houses from the 1940s through the 1960s. Pipe installed then is often galvanized steel or early copper, and it is now decades past the age when failures begin.

The city also publishes a lead service line inventory, which covers the pipe from the main to your meter. That is separate from the lines inside your house. A repipe replaces the house plumbing, and the inventory page tells you what is known about the service line that feeds it.

What affects the cost of a repipe

  • House sizeMore fixtures and more pipe mean more labor and material.
  • Material choicePEX and copper differ in material and install time.
  • AccessCrawlspaces and open basements are easier than slab homes or finished walls.
  • Wall repairPatching and painting are separate from the plumbing.
  • PermitPermit rules vary, so ask the City of West Linn Building Division about your project.
