Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in West Linn, OR

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons in an hour. Burst pipe repair in West Linn starts with the main water valve: find it and turn it clockwise until it stops, and then call (503) 974-3329.

The emergency line is open 24/7. While the plumber is on the way, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, switch off power to wet areas and move valuables out of the water path.

Stop the water first

The main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the house, in a basement, crawlspace, garage or utility closet. If it is outside, it is in a meter box near the street and often needs a meter key. Close the valve until it stops. If you cannot find it, look at the water meter, since there is often a shutoff on the house side.

Once the water is off, open the lowest tap in the house to drain the pipes and ease pressure. Switch off the water heater so the elements do not run dry. Keep away from wet outlets, appliances and any area where water is near wiring.

Burst pipe checklist

Follow these steps in order.

  • Close the main water valve.
  • Open the lowest faucet to drain the line.
  • Switch off the water heater and any power to wet areas at the breaker.
  • Move belongings and furniture out of the water path.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurance claim, then call (503) 974-3329.

What causes a pipe to burst

Freezing is the classic cause, since water expands as it turns to ice and splits the pipe wall. But bursts also come from high pressure, old pipe material that has weakened, corrosion at joints and accidents such as a nail through a wall. The location of the break tells the plumber a lot about the cause.

A split on a freeze-prone exterior wall or in a crawlspace points to cold. A pinhole in a copper line points to corrosion. A joint that has blown apart points to pressure or a poor connection.

Cracked and broken supply pipe: the repair

A burst section is cut out and replaced, and the plumber checks the surrounding pipe to decide whether the failure is isolated. Copper repairs use new copper or push-fit connectors, and PEX repairs use crimp or expansion fittings. A pipe that has failed once in a corroded run often fails again next to the repair.

If the pipe is galvanized steel or aging copper, the plumber may recommend extending the repair or discussing repiping. The goal is to restore water without leaving a weak link that fails again.

Water service line leaks

The service line is the buried pipe from the meter to the house. A break there shows as wet ground, a sunken patch in the yard or a meter that runs with the house shut off. Repair means locating the break, digging and replacing the section, and some jobs call for a longer replacement of the line.

Cold snaps and old pipe in West Linn homes

West Linn homes include a lot of mid-century housing. The city's history page shows the population jumped from 2,923 in 1960 to more than 7,000 in 1970, so many houses date from those years. Plumbing from that period was often installed before modern pressure regulation and material standards, and the pipes behind the walls are now several decades old.

Exterior wall pipes, crawlspace lines and hose bibs are the first to fail in a cold snap. If a freeze is forecast, disconnect hoses, open cabinet doors under exterior sinks and let a faucet drip. The city's water reports show the supply is treated river water, so freezing is a house problem, not a supply problem.
